The DIKW Pyramid: The Backbone of All Thinking

Every great prompt, lesson, and AI workflow rests on one quiet idea: information only becomes valuable when it climbs the DIKW pyramid. Understanding this ladder is the single most important skill for anyone building with AI — because it tells you exactly what to ask for and why.

DataRaw, unprocessed facts — "7.2", "Nigerian", "10:04am"
InformationData given context — "The exam started at 10:04am in Lagos."
KnowledgePatterns & rules — "Lagos students perform best in morning sessions."
WisdomJudgment & action — "Schedule live classes at 10am to boost pass rates."
Why it matters for AI

Large Language Models are information machines — they turn data into information and knowledge. They do not produce wisdom by themselves. Your job as the prompt-writer is to supply the judgment at the top of the pyramid: the constraints, the purpose, the audience.

Analogy: The Library, The Librarian, The Mentor

"Data is a warehouse of books. Information is the catalogue. Knowledge is the librarian who has read them all. Wisdom is the mentor who knows which book to hand you — and why — at exactly the right moment."

An AI model is the all-knowing librarian. It has the catalogue memorised. But you are the mentor. Only you know the student, the deadline, the goal, and the stakes. That is why prompt-writing is really wisdom transfer, not just query-writing.

Applying DIKW to a real lesson

LevelExample (Graphic Design course)Prompting move
Data"Colour hex #FF6B6B, 72 DPI, 1080×1080"Give exact, structured facts.
Information"This is a warm red for a brand logo."Attach meaning & context.
Knowledge"Warm reds increase urgency and appetite."Link to established principles.
Wisdom"Use warm red for the CTA so cart abandonment drops."Decide action with intent.

The Prompt-Writing Framework: Ask Like a Mentor

A powerful prompt is not a single magic sentence — it is a five-part structure that hands the model your context, your goal, and your standards. Memorise this as the R.O.L.E. model:

LetterComponentWhat to includeExample
RRoleThe persona the model should adopt"You are a senior UX copywriter."
OObjectiveThe exact deliverable & purpose"Write a 150-word landing hero."
LLimitsConstraints: length, tone, format"Plain language, no jargon, 2 sentences max."
EEvidenceFacts, examples, or reference material"It is for a fitness app for teenagers."
The X rule

Always add an exemplar ("Here is a great example: …") and an exit check ("Before you finish, confirm you used the word 'motion' at least once."). One shows the machine the target; the other forces it to verify its own work.

From vague to precise — a live example

"Write about AI." → "You are a teacher explaining prompt engineering to 14-year-olds. Write a 120-word analogy comparing a prompt to an order at a restaurant. Use the word 'kitchen' and end with one question to the student."

The second request carries role, objective, limits, evidence, and an exit check. Notice how the model now has everything it needs to produce a usable first draft, not a generic lecture.

Six reusable prompt patterns

  1. Chain-of-Thought: "Reason step-by-step before answering."
  2. Few-Shot: Provide 2–3 worked examples, then ask for a new one.
  3. Persona: Assign a specific expert role to control tone.
  4. Constraint: "No bullet points. Write in full paragraphs."
  5. Self-Critique: "Now review your own answer and list 3 weaknesses."
  6. Structured Output: "Return a JSON object with these keys."

Multi-Modal Workflows: Teaching with Every Sense

Modern AI is not text-only. A complete instructional workflow moves through text → image → audio → video, each step reinforcing the last. This mirrors how the human brain actually encodes memory: words build structure, images build association, audio builds rhythm, and video builds rehearsal.

The four-lane pipeline

LaneTool familyLearner benefitExample prompt
TextLLMsConcept & structure"Draft a lesson outline on colour theory."
ImageImage generatorsVisual association"Create a 4-panel diagram of the colour wheel."
AudioTTS / voice AIMemory & rhythm"Narrate the outline in a calm teacher voice."
VideoAI editingRehearsal & engagement"Turn the narrations into a 60s explainer."

Analogy: Recipe, Ingredients, Chef, Service

"Text is the recipe. Image is the ingredients photo. Audio is the chef's commentary. Video is the finished dish being served."

Each lane alone is incomplete. The recipe without the photo is confusing; the photo without the recipe is decoration. The full multi-modal workflow gives the student the whole meal — structure, visuals, sound, and demonstration.

Build an asset in 4 prompts

  1. Script: "Write a 4-beat script for 'How to compress video' for beginners."
  2. Storyboard: "Describe 4 frames that match each beat."
  3. Visuals: "Illustrate frame 2: a timeline bar being squashed."
  4. Narration: "Record a 20s voiceover of the script."
Teacher tip

Always let the learner manipulate one lane themselves — e.g. re-prompt the image in a new style. Passive watching builds recall; active building builds mastery.

Section 04

Certification Exam Solutions

This section shows the exam-style questions for the AI & Instructional Design track, with model answers. No answer key is stored — internalise the reasoning, then apply it.

Q1. Explain the DIKW pyramid and give one example of each level in a prompt context.

Model answer: The DIKW pyramid orders raw input into value: Data (raw, unlabelled facts), Information (data with context), Knowledge (patterns and rules), and Wisdom (sound judgment applied to action). Example for a prompt on ad copy: Data = "Red, 24, Lagos"; Information = "A young Lagos audience"; Knowledge = "Short red CTAs convert well with Gen-Z"; Wisdom = "Write a 6-word red CTA for a Lagos Gen-Z audience." A good prompt supplies at least the top two levels so the model can act.

Q2. What are the four parts you would include to turn a vague request into a high-quality prompt?

Model answer: Use the R.O.L.E. framework — Role (assign an expert persona), Objective (define the exact deliverable), Limits (constrain tone, length, and format), and Evidence (supply facts, examples, or an exemplar). Optionally add a fifth: an Exit check so the model verifies its own output.

Q3. Describe one multi-modal workflow and explain why the learner benefits from all four lanes.

Model answer: A text → image → audio → video pipeline: script the lesson (text), storyboard it (image), narrate it (audio), then edit it into a short explainer (video). The learner benefits because each lane encodes the content differently — language builds structure, imagery builds association, narration builds rhythm and recall, and video builds rehearsal. Combined, they activate multiple memory systems, so the concept sticks.

Q4. Why is "wisdom transfer" a better way to describe expert prompt-writing than "query-writing"?

Model answer: A query simply asks for information. An expert prompt hands the model the context, constraints, purpose, and audience — the judgment an expert would apply. That is not just retrieving answers; it is transferring the reasoning that turns knowledge into a wise decision. The model supplies knowledge; the prompt-writer supplies wisdom.
